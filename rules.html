The Laws of Ash Creek

Every resident, traveller and outlaw is expected to understand the laws of the territory before entering Ash Creek.

These rules exist to protect fair play, meaningful storytelling and an enjoyable roleplay environment for everyone.

Chapter I

General Conduct

1.1

Respect Other Players

Treat all members of the community with respect, whether in-game or in community spaces. Harassment, discrimination, hate speech, or personal attacks will not be tolerated. Friendly banter is fine, but it must stop if another player asks you to stop.

1.2

Value the Community

Ash Creek RP is built on collaborative storytelling. Avoid behaviour that intentionally ruins another player's experience, even if it is technically allowed by the game. Winning should never come before creating enjoyable roleplay.

1.3

Listen to Staff

Staff decisions are made to keep the server fair and enjoyable. If you disagree with a decision, discuss it calmly through the appropriate channels rather than arguing in-game or publicly disrupting roleplay.

1.4

Exploiting Bugs

If you discover a bug or unintended mechanic, do not use it for personal gain. Report it to staff as soon as possible so it can be investigated and fixed.

1.5

Third-Party Software

The use of cheats, scripts, macros, or software that provides an unfair advantage is strictly prohibited. This includes anything that modifies gameplay in ways not intended by the server.

1.6

English Communication

To ensure everyone can understand roleplay, English should be used during public in-character interactions and official community channels. Private conversations in other languages are acceptable where appropriate, provided they are not used to exclude or gain an unfair advantage over others.

1.7

Common Sense

Not every situation can be covered by a written rule. Players are expected to use common sense and act in the spirit of fair, immersive roleplay. If an action would clearly damage the experience for others, it is probably not the right choice.

Chapter II

Roleplay Standards

2.1

Remain in Character

Remain in character at all times while in-game unless instructed otherwise by staff or a server system. Avoid discussing real-world topics or out-of-character information through voice or text that would break immersion.

2.2

Value Roleplay Over Winning

Ash Creek RP is a story-driven server, not a competition. Your goal should be to create believable and memorable interactions, even if your character loses, fails, or faces difficult consequences. Sometimes the best roleplay comes from setbacks rather than victories.

2.3

Create Believable Characters

Your character should behave in a way that is consistent with their personality, background, and the world around them. Avoid unrealistic actions that exist only to gain an advantage or disrupt the roleplay of others.

2.4

Value Your Character's Life

Your character should fear injury and death. Avoid reckless behaviour that a reasonable person would not attempt simply because there is a respawn system. Survival and self-preservation should influence your decisions whenever possible.

2.5

Value the Lives of Others

Remember that every player is telling their own story. Give other characters the opportunity to respond, negotiate, or react before escalating situations unnecessarily. Not every disagreement needs to end in violence.

2.6

Encourage Meaningful Roleplay

Take time to develop conversations, relationships, rivalries, and consequences. Avoid rushing interactions simply to reach an outcome. A slower, believable encounter is often more rewarding than a quick resolution.

2.7

Roleplay Injuries Realistically

If your character is injured, roleplay those injuries appropriately. The severity of your injuries should influence how your character behaves until they have received suitable medical treatment or enough time has passed for recovery.

2.8

Keep Roleplay Fair

Do not force another player's character into actions without giving them the opportunity to respond through roleplay. Allow interactions to develop naturally rather than assuming outcomes.

2.9

Respect the Setting

Ash Creek RP is set in the late 1800s American frontier. Characters, speech, clothing, and behaviour should reasonably fit the setting. While perfect historical accuracy is not expected, modern references, slang, memes, or unrealistic behaviour that breaks immersion should be avoided.

Chapter III

Character Rules

3.1

Create Original Characters

Every character should have their own unique identity, personality, and story. Do not create characters based on real people, celebrities, fictional characters, or historical figures. Ash Creek RP is about creating your own story, not recreating someone else's.

3.2

Appropriate Character Names

Character names must be realistic and appropriate for the late 1800s frontier setting. Joke names, offensive names, modern references, or names intended to break immersion are not permitted.

3.3

Separate Character Knowledge

Each character lives their own life. Information learned by one character must never be transferred to another character unless it is discovered naturally through roleplay. Every new character begins with their own experiences and knowledge.

3.4

Character Development

Allow your character to grow naturally over time. Friendships, rivalries, reputations, and personal goals should develop through roleplay rather than appearing instantly.

3.5

Permanent Character Decisions

Choices have consequences. Actions taken by your character should carry weight, whether they affect relationships, businesses, reputation, or standing within the community. Avoid treating serious events as if they never happened.

3.6

Character Death (CK)

Permanent Character Death (often referred to as Character Kill or CK) is entirely optional unless approved by both the player and staff. If you choose to permanently retire a character, that decision is final.

3.7

Character Switching

Do not switch between characters to gain an advantage, avoid consequences, or continue a situation from a different perspective. Each character should be treated as a completely separate individual.

3.8

Realistic Behaviour

Characters should behave in ways that are believable for their personality, occupation, and circumstances. While every character is unique, their actions should remain consistent with the world and the roleplay around them.

3.9

Flawed Characters Make Better Stories

Perfection is unrealistic. Characters are encouraged to have weaknesses, fears, bad habits, and personal struggles that make them feel believable. Not every gunslinger needs to be the fastest draw, and not every outlaw needs to win every encounter.

Chapter IV

Crime and Conflict

4.1

Crime Must Create Roleplay

Criminal activity is an important part of Ash Creek RP, but its purpose is to create engaging roleplay rather than easy profit or constant action. Every crime should provide meaningful interaction and opportunities for others to respond.

4.2

Escalation Before Violence

Violence should be a last resort, not the first option. Whenever reasonably possible, conflicts should begin with conversation, intimidation, negotiation, or other forms of roleplay before weapons are drawn. Immediate violence is only acceptable when it is clearly justified by the situation.

4.3

Rule of Five

To maintain fair and enjoyable roleplay, all criminal activities and hostile encounters are subject to a Rule of Five. No more than five players may actively participate on either side of a criminal act or hostile situation unless approved by staff or as part of a server-wide event. Players must not join an ongoing conflict after it has started if doing so would cause either side to exceed this limit.

4.4

No Random Violence

Attacking, kidnapping, robbing, or killing another player without meaningful roleplay or a believable in-character reason is prohibited. Every hostile action should have context and contribute to the story.

4.5

Accept the Consequences

If your character chooses a life of crime, they should also accept the risks that come with it. Arrest, injury, imprisonment, financial loss, or death are all natural consequences of criminal behaviour and should be roleplayed appropriately.

4.6

Fair Robberies

Robberies should focus on creating memorable interactions rather than simply taking another player's belongings. Victims must be given reasonable opportunities to respond through roleplay, and robberies should never be used solely to harass or repeatedly target the same individuals.

4.7

No Revenge Through Game Mechanics

Do not use game mechanics to immediately seek revenge after escaping, being treated, or returning from an encounter. Allow the story to progress naturally rather than treating every conflict as a cycle of retaliation.

4.8

Respect Event Cooldowns

Avoid repeatedly targeting the same person, business, or group in a short period simply because game mechanics allow it. Give situations time to develop so that roleplay remains varied and enjoyable for everyone involved.

4.9

Criminals and Lawmen Are Story Partners

Outlaws and law enforcement are not enemies outside of roleplay—they are partners in creating compelling stories. Both sides should aim to make encounters enjoyable, believable, and fair, regardless of the outcome.

4.10

Roleplay Over Victory

Do not treat criminal encounters as competitions that must be won at all costs. Whether you are an outlaw, deputy, or civilian, your priority should be creating enjoyable roleplay rather than securing a victory through game mechanics or technicalities.

Chapter V

New Life Rule

5.1

Being Knocked Unconscious

If your character is rendered unconscious but receives medical treatment and survives without respawning, they may retain memories of the incident. However, the effects of their injuries should be roleplayed appropriately. Confusion, memory gaps, dizziness, or other lingering effects are encouraged where they make sense for the severity of the incident.

5.2

Respawning

If you choose to respawn after becoming incapacitated, your character is considered to have no memory of the events that directly led to their incapacitation. You may not return to continue the situation, identify those involved, or use information gained during the encounter.

5.3

Returning to the Scene

After respawning, you must not return to the location of the incident with the intention of continuing or influencing the previous roleplay. Allow the situation to conclude naturally without your involvement. To reinforce this, you may not call or whistle for your horse for 15 minutes after respawning. This helps ensure you cannot immediately return to the scene or re-engage with the previous situation.

5.4

Respect the Story

The New Life Rule exists to preserve fair and immersive roleplay. Do not attempt to bypass it through other characters, friends, or out-of-character communication. If your character no longer remembers the event, they should not act upon information they could not reasonably know.

5.5

Medical Roleplay

If your character survives without respawning, roleplay appropriate recovery based on the injuries sustained. Serious injuries should not disappear immediately after treatment, and your character's behaviour should reflect the impact of the incident where reasonable.

Chapter VI

Metagaming and Powergaming

6.1

No Metagaming

Metagaming is the use of out-of-character (OOC) information to benefit your character in-game. Your character should only know information they have realistically learned through roleplay. Information gained from Discord, livestreams, friends, or other external sources must not influence your character's decisions. Examples of metagaming include: Using information from a Discord conversation your character was never part of. Watching another player's livestream to locate them. Acting on information shared in an out-of-character voice or text channel.

6.2

No Powergaming

Powergaming is forcing actions or outcomes that prevent another player from fairly participating in the roleplay. Roleplay should be collaborative. Give other players reasonable opportunities to react instead of deciding the outcome for them. Examples of powergaming include: Emoting that you instantly knock someone unconscious without allowing them to respond. Forcing another character to hand over information without giving them the chance to react. Performing unrealistic actions that ignore the limits of your character or the game world.

6.3

No Combat Logging

Disconnecting from the server to avoid roleplay, arrest, injury, robbery, or any other in-character consequence is strictly prohibited. If you unexpectedly disconnect during an active roleplay situation, you should make reasonable efforts to return and continue the interaction where possible.

6.4

Out-of-Character Communication

Out-of-character communication should be kept to a minimum while in-game and must never be used to gain an advantage. If OOC communication is necessary, keep it brief and ensure it does not interrupt or influence the ongoing roleplay.

6.5

Respect Fair Outcomes

Roleplay is built on cooperation, not control. Do not manipulate mechanics, emotes, or wording to guarantee success or deny another player the opportunity to contribute to the story. Sometimes your character will lose and that's part of telling a good story.

6.6

Use Common Sense

Not every example of metagaming or powergaming can be listed in the rules. If an action relies on information your character could not realistically know, or removes another player's ability to participate fairly, it is likely against the spirit of these rules.

Official Notice

Ignorance of the Law Is No Defence

By joining Ash Creek RP, you confirm that you have read and understood the server rules. Staff may take appropriate action when these standards are ignored.
