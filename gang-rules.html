Official Server Regulations

Gang Regulations

These regulations govern informal criminal groups, officially recognised gangs, organised criminal activity and gang-related hostilities throughout Ash Creek.

These are out-of-character server rules. Their contents do not automatically represent information known by any character.

Chapter I

Groups and Official Recognition

1.1

Informal Groups

Players may form informal groups, travel together and take part in criminal roleplay without first receiving staff approval.

1.2

Freedom to Roam

Informal groups may roam throughout Ash Creek, build relationships, establish an identity and develop their story naturally through roleplay.

1.3

Official Recognition

A group wishing to become an officially recognised gang must open a support ticket and provide any information requested by server staff.

1.4

Recognition Is Not Automatic

Opening a ticket does not guarantee approval. Staff may consider the group's roleplay quality, activity, leadership, history and overall suitability before granting recognition.

1.5

Limits of Informal Groups

Informal groups may take part in ordinary criminal roleplay but may not enter a formal gang war, claim recognised-gang privileges or present themselves as staff-approved.

1.6

Leadership Responsibility

The leader of a recognised gang is responsible for setting expectations, managing members and addressing behaviour that damages fair or meaningful roleplay.

1.7

Meaningful Membership

Gang membership must be established through roleplay. Players may not be added temporarily or recruited immediately before an activity solely to increase a group's numbers.

Chapter II

The Rule of Five

2.1

Maximum Participation

No more than five players from the same group, gang or cooperating side may actively participate in a criminal activity or hostile situation.

2.2

Activities Covered

The Rule of Five applies to robberies, kidnappings, ambushes, raids, organised theft, extortion, retaliations, gang disputes and other coordinated criminal scenes.

2.3

Support Roles Count

Scouts, lookouts, drivers, transport, hired gunmen, associates and any other player providing active assistance count toward the same limit of five.

2.4

Allies Share the Same Limit

Allied or cooperating groups do not receive separate allowances. All players working together during the same situation count toward one shared limit of five.

2.5

No Reinforcement Rotation

Players may not rotate in and out of a continuing scene to bypass the limit. Once five members are involved, additional players may not join as reinforcements.

2.6

No Team Splitting

A group may not divide into separate teams to attack the same target, opposing group or location as though they were unrelated incidents.

2.7

Declared Wars

The Rule of Five remains in effect during a formal gang war unless server staff clearly approve a different limit beforehand.

Chapter III

Criminal Conduct

3.1

Roleplay Before Winning

Criminal activities should create enjoyable and continuing roleplay. Escaping, obtaining money or defeating another group must not be treated as more important than the quality of the scene.

3.2

Reasonable Escalation

Criminal violence should be proportionate to the circumstances. Minor disagreements should not immediately become repeated or lethal attacks.

3.3

Genuine Hostages

Hostages must be genuine participants in the situation. Friends, associates or gang members may not pretend to be unwilling hostages in order to gain an advantage.

3.4

Hostage Treatment

Hostages must be given reasonable opportunities to roleplay and may not be subjected to excessive confinement, unnecessary humiliation or behaviour intended only to upset the player.

3.5

Reasonable Demands

Demands made during robberies or hostage scenes must be proportionate and achievable. Players should not make impossible demands simply to force negotiations to fail.

3.6

Territory and Public Areas

Groups may develop influence over an area through roleplay, but may not permanently block access to towns, roads, businesses or other public locations.

3.7

Repeated Targeting

Players must not repeatedly target the same person, group, business or location without allowing reasonable time and new roleplay between incidents.

3.8

Combat Logging

Players may not disconnect, respawn or otherwise leave the game to avoid the consequences of an active criminal or hostile situation.

Chapter IV

Gang Conflict and Formal Wars

4.1

Meaningful Cause

Gang conflict must arise from meaningful in-character events. Hostility should contribute to an ongoing story rather than exist solely to create combat.

4.2

Natural Escalation

Conflict should escalate through roleplay. Warnings, threats, negotiation and proportionate retaliation should be considered before a dispute becomes an organised war.

4.3

Eligibility for Formal War

Only officially recognised gangs may take part in a formal gang war.

4.4

War Ticket

A recognised gang wishing to begin a formal war must open the appropriate support ticket before organised hostilities begin.

4.5

Terms and Objectives

Staff may require the groups involved to agree upon objectives, limitations, duration, victory conditions and terms for ending the conflict.

4.6

Third-Party Interference

Uninvolved gangs or groups may not enter an active conflict merely to strengthen one side, obtain easy violence or alter the outcome.

4.7

Respecting Outcomes

Surrenders, peace agreements and concluded wars must be respected. A completed conflict may not be immediately restarted without new and meaningful roleplay.

Chapter V

Information and Administration

5.1

Character Knowledge

Information learned through Discord, websites, streams, tickets or out-of-character conversation may not be used by a character unless it has also been learned through legitimate roleplay.

5.2

Identifying Gang Members

Clothing, colours, horses, voices or usual companions may contribute to suspicion, but do not automatically prove a character's identity or gang membership.

5.3

External Coordination

Players may not use private messages, streams or external voice channels to coordinate an active in-character criminal or hostile scene.

5.4

Leadership and Identity Changes

Recognised gangs must report significant changes to their leadership, name or structure through the appropriate ticket system.

5.5

Inactivity and Recognition

A gang that becomes inactive for an extended period may have its official recognition reviewed or withdrawn.

5.6

Loss of Recognition

Staff may withdraw recognition where a gang repeatedly breaches server rules, fails to maintain suitable leadership or no longer operates as an active roleplay group.

5.7

Reporting Concerns

Rule concerns should be reported through the proper support process. Players should avoid pausing or derailing an active scene to argue over rules unless immediate intervention is genuinely required.

5.8

Staff Discretion

Staff may intervene where behaviour undermines fair, believable or enjoyable roleplay, even where a situation is not described word-for-word within these regulations.

Final Notice

Roleplay Comes First

Gang roleplay should create lasting stories, meaningful consequences and opportunities for everyone involved. These regulations must never be treated as permission to prioritise winning over fair and believable roleplay.
